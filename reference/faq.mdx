## What is Coherence Atlas?

Coherence Atlas is Coherence Systems' developing product for investigating how complex systems change: what continues to hold, what shifts, and what may be reorganizing.

## What do persistence, drift, degradation, and transition mean?

These terms help frame different questions about change:

- **Persistence:** The pattern or function you care about continues across the period and conditions being examined. Individual readings can vary while something important still holds.
- **Drift:** A gradual change relative to a stated point of comparison.
- **Degradation:** A worsening of the performance or capacity that matters for the question. Changing readings alone do not establish degradation.
- **Transition:** A shift in how a system behaves or is organized. Understanding the shift requires context and evidence.

For a hypothetical example, imagine airflow readings in a building gradually changing. Occupancy, sensor placement, and equipment condition could each help explain the change. The readings raise a drift question; deciding whether performance has worsened requires more evidence.

## What does “time, scale, and state” mean?

- **Time:** How observations and behavior change over a period.
- **Scale:** The level of detail used to examine the system, such as a short interval versus a longer pattern, or a component versus the wider system.
- **State:** The system's condition or configuration in the description being used.

These views help frame comparisons. The useful choices depend on the system and the question.

## What is a structured review?

Start with a clear question, identify what was observed and under which conditions, choose a relevant comparison, and explain what the evidence supports. Keep open questions visible so the next step can address them.

For a potential evaluation, this means agreeing on what you want to learn and what would count as useful evidence. [Explore a potential fit](/guides/applied-pilots).

## Who is this work for?

Researchers, technical teams reviewing operational change, and potential partners exploring scoped evaluations. You can start with a question about your system or workflow.
